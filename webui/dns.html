How it works

Filtered DNS in three steps

Your device asks this resolver where a domain lives. If the domain is an ad, a tracker or malicious, the request is answered with nothing, so the bad content never loads.

1

Point your device here

Add the DNS-over-HTTPS or DNS-over-TLS endpoint in your device or router settings.

2

Queries get filtered

Each lookup is checked against up-to-date blocklists for ads, trackers and threats.

3

Browse clean

Pages load without ads and tracking, and everything travels over an encrypted connection.

On the TunGuard VPN

Connect through the VPN and filtering just happens

If you reach this server through its WireGuard VPN, the operator can switch filtering on for your device without you changing a single setting. Your DNS is routed through this resolver automatically, so ads and trackers are blocked on every device in the group.

1

Get a VPN profile

The server owner generates a WireGuard configuration for your device and sends you the QR code or file.

2

Join a filtered group

From the TunGuard dashboard the owner moves your device into a policy group that has AdGuard DNS filtering turned on.

3

Browsing gets filtered

The server quietly sends your device's DNS requests through the filtered resolver. No app to install, no per-device configuration.

Why this is handy

Because the filtering happens at the network level, a device that is already set up is covered the moment it joins a filtered group, and it stops being covered when it leaves. The owner runs the whole thing from the dashboard: create a policy group, turn on AdGuard DNS filtering, then move devices in or out. Every device in the group is answered through the same blocklists.

A device using its own Private DNS (DNS-over-HTTPS or DNS-over-TLS) takes that path instead, so point it at the endpoints on this page manually.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What is DNS, and what does a resolver do? ▾

DNS is the address book of the Internet: it turns a name like example.com into the numeric address your device connects to. A resolver is the server that answers those lookups.

Which endpoints can I use? ▾

DNS-over-HTTPS at your /dns-query URL, and DNS-over-TLS on the hostname shown at the top of this page. Both lead to the same filtered resolver.

Do I need an account? ▾

No. The resolver is open and needs no sign-in. There is nothing to install on your devices beyond pointing them at the endpoints.

Are my queries private? ▾

Traffic between your device and this resolver is encrypted with DoH or DoT. It is a filtering resolver, so treat it like any third-party DNS: it exists to block unwanted content, not to profile you.

What ports does it use? ▾

DNS-over-HTTPS uses the standard HTTPS port (443). DNS-over-TLS uses the port shown next to the DoT endpoint above (853 by default).

Will this block everything? ▾

It blocks ad, tracker and malicious domains from its blocklists. Some apps can bypass DNS filtering by talking directly to an address; those are not affected.
